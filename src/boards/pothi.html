पत्र · how to read a leaf

Seven things to look for on a leaf.

  1. १पत्रThe leaf
  2. २पत्राङ्कThe leaf number
  3. ३मङ्गलThe opening blessing
  4. ४चित्रThe painting
  5. ५हाशियाThe margin title
  6. ६पाठThe text
  7. ७श्लोकाङ्कThe verse number

१ · पत्र · patra

A pothi is a stack of loose leaves.

It is not bound like a book. The leaves are wider than they are tall, kept flat between two boards and wrapped in cloth. This painted leaf opens one.

२ · पत्राङ्क · patrāṅka

Leaves are counted, not pages.

A leaf usually carries one number for both its sides. A later hand has added a count in pencil along the top edge: “TOTAL FOLIO-162”.

३ · मङ्गल · maṅgala

It opens with a blessing, in red.

Before the first word of the work, the scribe salutes a deity. The first line here seems to read ॐ श्रीगणेशाय नमः, a salutation to Gaṇeśa.

Reading to be confirmed by the family

४ · चित्र · citra

Some opening leaves are painted.

A chariot drawn by white horses sits in the middle of the text, and gold scrollwork runs round the border. Most leaves have no picture at all.

५ · हाशिया · hāśiyā

The margin carries a short title.

Loose leaves get separated. A short name in the margin, here what looks like गी. टी., tells a reader which work a stray leaf belongs to.

Reading to be confirmed by the family

६ · पाठ · pāṭha

Most leaves are text, inside ruled lines.

A plain leaf from further in, numbered 58 in pencil. The scribe ruled a frame first, in red and yellow, then wrote thirteen lines with no spaces between the words.

७ · श्लोकाङ्क · ślokāṅka

Each verse ends with its number.

Two upright strokes, the double daṇḍa, close a verse, with its number beside them: here, २८ ॥. It is how a reader keeps their place.
